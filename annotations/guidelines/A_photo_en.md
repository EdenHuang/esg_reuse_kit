# Annotation A: is this a photo? (guideline v1.5, 2026-10-18) — English translation

English translation (2026-10-28) of `A_photo_zh.md`, the guideline the two student annotators used. The Chinese
version is authoritative. "Dr Huang" is the author (Eden).

## What you see
One image cut from a company's sustainability report.

## What to answer
- **Photo** (press 1): a real-world photograph taken with a camera.
- **Not a photo** (press 2): a drawn, designed or computer-made image.
- If you hesitate, also tick **Not sure** (press 3). You still have to choose photo or not a photo.

## Before you start
- Dr Huang sends you a zip file. Unzip it and open `index.html`. **Use a browser on a laptop or desktop, not a
  phone.** Keep the files in the same folder.
1. Read the example page first: real images with the correct answer and a one-line reason.
2. Do 20 practice items. After each answer you see the correct answer and the reason. You need 18 correct to
   start the real items; otherwise read the examples again and do another set of practice items.

## Counts as a photo
- People: group photos, portraits, people at work or at an event.
- Places and things: buildings, factories, machines, products, landscapes, vehicles.
- A product shot on a plain background.
- Satellite and aerial photographs.
- A photo with some text or graphics laid over it, as long as the photo is still the main content.
- A collage made of several photos.
- **An incomplete photo is still a photo**: only part of a photo (cut into tiles, half cropped away, cut by a
  round or slanted frame) counts as "photo" as long as you can see that a camera took it (real light and shadow,
  texture, objects). You judge the content, not the completeness.

## Does not count as a photo
- Charts, tables, infographics, flowcharts, timelines.
- Illustrations, cartoons, icons, logos, decorative patterns or background art.
- Maps (satellite and aerial photographs do count as photos).
- A scan of a document or certificate, where the whole image is that document.
- Screenshots of websites or apps.
- 3D renderings or computer-generated images that clearly do not look camera-made.
- Mostly text or a designed banner with only a faint photo in the background.

## Hard cases
- Someone holding a certificate at the scene: photo. The certificate alone: not a photo.
- Cannot tell a real photo from a 3D rendering: judge by appearance and tick Not sure.
- Too small or too blurred: judge by what you can see and tick Not sure if needed.
- Only a small fragment is left and you cannot tell what it shows (a patch of sky, a piece of wall): choose
  "photo" if you can see the texture of a real object, "not a photo" if it looks like a flat colour or a
  gradient, and tick Not sure.

## Doing a batch
- Each batch is one file of about 55 images, one per page. Press 1 or 2 (and 3 if you hesitate), then Enter for
  the next image. ← goes back to change an answer. You can only move on after answering.
- Progress is saved in the browser: close and reopen (same computer, same browser) to continue.
- After the last image, press "Download answers" and e-mail the downloaded file to Dr Huang at once, without
  changing its name or content. You can go on to the next batch.

## Quality checks
- Some items have known answers and are used to check quality. If too many are wrong, Dr Huang will ask you to
  redo that batch and send you a new file.
- The time spent on each item is recorded too. Take as long as you need.

## Rules
- Judge from the image only; do not search the web.
- Do not discuss items with the other annotator.
- About 5 seconds per image. Rest whenever you like.
- Use a browser on a laptop or desktop, not a phone.
- Ask Dr Huang if anything is unclear.
